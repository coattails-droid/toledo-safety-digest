- Toyota Land Cruiser 2024-2027 - fuel leak, fire risk (ongoing; Toyota Oct 14)
  - What happened: a fuel tube connection near the fuel tank can come apart, letting fuel leak until the hybrid system shuts the engine off. Leaking fuel near an ignition source raises fire risk. About 106,000 U.S. vehicles.
  - Models: certain 2024, 2025, 2026 and 2027 Toyota Land Cruiser (hybrid).
  - Remedy: dealers inspect the connection and add a supplemental clamp, or replace the resin fuel tube and add the clamp if needed, free. Owner letters by mid-December 2026.
  - Toyota's notice doesn't include a do-not-drive or park-outside instruction. If you smell fuel or see a leak under the vehicle, don't drive it; call a dealer.
  - NHTSA campaign number: still not posted on NHTSA's site (checked Oct 17) (search results pointing to 26V124 are an older campaign; not used).
  - Check your VIN: https://www.toyota.com/recall or https://www.nhtsa.gov/recalls ; Toyota 1-800-331-4331.
  - Sources: https://pressroom.toyota.com/toyota-recalls-certain-my2024-2027-land-cruiser-vehicles/

- Kids / Poison Control
  - Concentrated 7-OH kratom products (NEW to this digest; Michigan Poison & Drug Information Center, Oct 9 fall bulletin)
    - What: products sold at gas stations, smoke shops, convenience stores and online as "kratom," "wellness" or energy products can contain concentrated 7-hydroxymitragynine (7-OH), which has stronger opioid-like effects. They come as powders, capsules, gummies, teas and liquid shots and can look like candy or supplements. A Michigan health department bulletin counted 69 kratom exposures involving children in 2025, and 7-OH-enhanced products carried higher overdose risk and more ICU care. Labels may not match contents.
    - What to do: keep these products away from children; ask teens about them. If someone is very sleepy or breathing slowly, call 911. Poison Help 1-800-222-1222 (24/7).
    - Source: https://www.poison.med.wayne.edu/poison-periodical-fall-26/kratom-and-concentrated-7-oh-products-what-you-need-to-know
  - Heads-up: National Lead Poisoning Prevention Week is Oct 25-31 ("Keep Kids Safe from Lead"). Homes built before 1978, lead service lines and old faucets are common sources; ask your child's doctor about a blood lead test. https://www.msms.org/news/national-lead-poisoning-prevention-week
  - Fake "Cat's Claw" supplements laced with kratom-derived opioids (new to this digest; Washington Poison Center alert updated Oct 7)
    - What: poison centers across the U.S. are treating overdoses and severe withdrawal from products labeled "Cat's Claw" that contain concentrated 7-OH, MGM-15 and MGPI (opioids derived from kratom). Utah Poison Control reported four confirmed deaths. As of Oct 7, Buzzers and Homiez brands were the ones implicated by investigations; sold in vape shops, gas stations, convenience stores and online. The DEA made MGM-15 and MGPI Schedule I in August 2026.
    - What to do: stop using cat's claw products from those outlets; keep them away from children; dispose of them. If someone is extremely sleepy or breathing slowly, call 911 and give naloxone. Poison Help 1-800-222-1222.
    - Source: https://www.wapc.org/insights/safety-alert-fake-cats-claw/

    - Large doses of diphenhydramine can cause hallucinations, seizures, dangerous heart rhythms, coma and death. Talk with teens; lock up medicines including OTC. Poison Control 1-800-222-1222; 911 for breathing trouble, hard-to-wake or seizures.
