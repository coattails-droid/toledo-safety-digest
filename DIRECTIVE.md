## What to check

Check the last 24 to 48 hours, and carry forward anything still active.

1. **Recalls.** Food and product recalls affecting the Toledo area or sold at stores there: FDA, USDA FSIS, CPSC, Ohio Department of Agriculture, and retailer notices.
2. **Hazmat.** Chemical or fuel spills and leaks, gasoline odors, truck hazmat incidents, industrial releases, and evacuations or shelter-in-place orders in Toledo, Lucas County, and the nearby area.
3. **Gas leaks.** Natural gas leaks, line strikes during digging or construction, gas-related explosions or fires, evacuations, and service outages in Toledo and the surrounding area (Lucas and Wood counties, Maumee, Sylvania, Perrysburg, Rossford, Oregon). Include pipeline incidents in northwest Ohio and southeast Michigan, plus PHMSA or PUCO notices.
   - Sources: Columbia Gas of Ohio, Toledo Fire and Rescue, suburban fire departments, PUCO, PHMSA, and local news.
   - Give the location, evacuations or road closures, whether it's resolved, and the cause if known.
4. **Rail.** Derailments, rail hazmat releases, and serious rail incidents in the Toledo area, northwest Ohio, and southeast Michigan (Norfolk Southern, CSX, Canadian National, Ann Arbor Railroad). Include NTSB or FRA updates, and any major hazmat derailment elsewhere in Ohio or the Great Lakes.
   - Give what spilled (if anything), evacuations, and road or crossing closures.
5. **Water and crops.** The Toledo drinking water system, boil or do-not-drink advisories, and crop or livestock contamination.
   - Be skeptical of stories that recycle the 2014 Toledo water crisis as if new. Verify with the City of Toledo or a major outlet before reporting any drinking-water order.
6. **Water mains and sewers.** Water main breaks, sewer and storm line collapses, sinkholes from failed pipes, overflows or basement backups, and the resulting boil advisories, no-water areas, and street closures in Toledo's water service area.
   - Sources: Toledo Department of Public Utilities, the Lucas County Sanitary Engineer, Northwestern Water and Sewer District, and local news.
   - Give the location, the effect, and the expected repair time.
7. **Maumee River** (named priority, one line every day). Spills, sewage overflows, chemical releases, fish kills, algal blooms reaching Maumee Bay, Ohio EPA and Army Corps notices (including Toledo Harbor dredging, Sep to Dec 2026), and recreation or drinking-water advisories along the river, including the Swan Creek E. coli advisory while it's in effect.
8. **Lake Erie and EPA notices** (one line every day). This covers the western basin and the Toledo area.
   - The algal bloom: the latest NOAA Lake Erie harmful algal bloom bulletin, how far it extends, and toxin (microcystin) levels.
   - Microcystin testing at Toledo's Collins Park plant and its Lake Erie intake.
   - Beach advisories.
   - Spills and sewage overflows into the lake.
   - Fish kills and fish consumption advice.
   - Low-oxygen dead zones (hypoxia).
   - Storm, seiche, and high-water warnings.
   - New notices affecting western Lake Erie from U.S. EPA Region 5 (including its Great Lakes restoration program), Ohio EPA, or Michigan EGLE: enforcement actions, violations, permits, public comment periods, H2Ohio updates, phosphorus limit plans, and dredging.
9. **Invasive species.** New detections, quarantines, and control work in the Toledo area, northwest Ohio, Lake Erie, and southeast Michigan.
   - On land: spotted lanternfly, spongy moth, emerald ash borer, Asian longhorned beetle, box tree moth, hemlock woolly adelgid, and beech leaf disease.
   - In the water: invasive carp, round goby, zebra and quagga mussels, sea lamprey, hydrilla, and starry stonewort.
   - Plants: phragmites and Japanese knotweed when there's local action.
   - Sources: Ohio Department of Agriculture, Ohio DNR, USDA APHIS, U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service, Ohio Sea Grant, Michigan DNR, Toledo Metroparks, and local news.
   - Say what was found, where, what residents should do, and any quarantine rules.
10. **Air** (one line every day).
    - (a) Today's and tomorrow's air quality index (AQI) from AirNow, ozone action days, the main pollutant, and wildfire smoke.
    - (b) Hazardous materials in the air: refinery or industrial releases and flaring (the Toledo and Oregon refineries), chemical plumes, toxic smoke, shelter-in-place orders, and air violation notices.
    - (c) Mold: the outdoor spore count or forecast, and any mold advisories from the health department after flooding. If no count is published, say so rather than guessing.
11. **Kids / Poison Control.** New warnings about children's products from America's Poison Centers, Ohio's poison centers, and the Michigan Poison and Drug Information Center.
    - Examples: button batteries, magnets, laundry pods, cannabis or nicotine products that look like candy, liquid nicotine, toys or cosmetics with lead, and viral challenges.
    - Pair these with any related new CPSC or FDA children's recalls.
    - Name the product, the hazard, and what parents should do.
12. **Lawsuits.** Class actions and mass tort filings, settlements, and claim deadlines involving Toledo-area residents, local businesses, employers, utilities, or landlords, or tied to the topics above.
    - Sources: Toledo Blade, WTOL, 13abc, the Northern District of Ohio federal court, ClassAction.org, Top Class Actions, and settlement administrator sites.
    - Say who qualifies and any claim deadline.
    - List real, verifiable filings only. Give no legal advice.
13. **Roadwork.** Construction, lane and ramp closures, detours, and bridge work in Toledo and the suburbs, and on I-75, I-475/US-23, I-280, the Ohio Turnpike, US-24, and the Anthony Wayne, Skyway, and MLK bridges.
    - Sources: ODOT District 2 and OHGO, the City of Toledo's weekly closure list, the Lucas County Engineer, and the Turnpike.
    - Put closures starting or changing within 7 days first. Give the location, what's closed, dates and hours, and the detour.
14. **DUI checkpoints.** Officially announced sobriety (OVI) checkpoints and saturation patrols in Lucas and Wood counties and nearby within 7 days.
    - Sources: Toledo Police, the county sheriffs, the State Highway Patrol, the Lucas County OVI Task Force, and suburban police.
    - Give the date, hours, and general location as announced. Never guess.
15. **Protests & demonstrations.** Publicly announced or trending protests, marches, rallies, strikes, and picket lines in the Toledo area (including UT and BGSU) within 7 days.
    - Sources: public social posts and event pages found through web search, local news, and city and police notices.
    - Give the cause in neutral terms, the date, time, place or route, expected size, and any road closures or police advisories.
    - Stay neutral. Don't name or profile individuals. Label anything that comes only from unverified social posts. Use public content only.
16. **Sky.** Notable events visible from Toledo within 7 days: eclipses, meteor shower peaks, supermoons, bright planet pairings or oppositions, comets, strong aurora forecasts that reach northern Ohio (NOAA Space Weather Prediction Center, Kp 6 or higher), exceptional space station passes, equinoxes and solstices, and novae.
    - Give the time in ET, where to look, whether it's visible to the naked eye, and the cloud outlook.
    - Verify every date with NASA, NOAA, timeanddate.com, Sky & Telescope, or EarthSky.

20. **Missing persons and missing children.** Officially issued alerts and police requests for help finding missing people in Toledo, Lucas and Wood counties, and nearby northwest Ohio and southeast Michigan (Monroe County), from the last 7 days, plus any still-active AMBER Alert or Endangered Missing Adult Alert affecting the area.
    - Sources: Ohio AMBER Alert, Ohio Endangered Missing Adult and Missing Child alerts, the Ohio Attorney General's Missing Persons Unit, Toledo Police, the Lucas and Wood County sheriffs, suburban police, Michigan State Police, NCMEC, and local news reporting police requests.
    - For each person, give what police released: name, age, physical description and clothing, when and where last seen, any vehicle, whether police call them endangered, and the phone number to call (or 911). Include the official source link.
    - An active AMBER Alert or endangered missing person in the area goes in Needs attention. Others go in Worth knowing.
    - When police report someone found, say so once ("found safe" or "located" as officially worded) and then drop them. Share only what police published. Don't speculate about circumstances, and don't include unverified social posts.

### Emerging Tech Watch (sections 17 to 19)

For each of these, cover new developments from the last 24 to 48 hours and flag anything specific to Ohio, Michigan, or the Great Lakes first. Use neutral, factual wording. Separate confirmed developments (official records, filings, standards documents, peer-reviewed work, credible reporting) from claims such as "chemtrail" allegations or speculative health and conspiracy theories. Label anything that rests only on social media or unverified sources with a short reliability note, and never present it as fact. Watch for old stories recirculated as new.

17. **Geoengineering.** Solar radiation management (stratospheric aerosol injection, marine cloud brightening, cirrus thinning, space reflectors), cloud seeding and NOAA weather-modification reports, large-scale carbon removal (ocean alkalinity enhancement, iron fertilization, direct air capture at scale), field trials and startups, government and peer-reviewed studies and funding, federal and state bills to ban or regulate it, international moratoria, UN and Convention on Biological Diversity actions, and lawsuits.
18. **Smart dust.** Networks of tiny wireless sensors (MEMS motes, neural dust, sprayable or ingestible micro-sensors, nanosensor networks): research papers, commercial products and deployments, defense programs and contracts (DARPA, DoD), medical uses, health and safety findings, privacy, surveillance, and civil-liberties concerns, and regulation (FCC, FDA, state privacy laws, EU rules).
19. **6G cellular.** Standards work (3GPP Release 20/21, ITU IMT-2030), spectrum decisions and auctions (FCC, NTIA, upper mid-band, sub-THz), carrier and equipment-maker trials and rollouts (Verizon, AT&T, T-Mobile, Ericsson, Nokia, Samsung, Qualcomm), government funding and strategy, research breakthroughs, RF exposure studies, FCC exposure limits and lawsuits, privacy and surveillance (including sensing and positioning features built into 6G), and tower and small-cell permits or new wireless sites in Toledo, Lucas County, Ohio, or Michigan.

## How to prioritize and lay it out

C T asked on Sep 24, 2026 for the briefing to be prioritized at the assistant's discretion.

- **Rank by impact on health, safety, home, or plans today.**
  - Top priority is anything that needs action now: drinking-water orders or boil advisories, shelter-in-place orders or evacuations, active nearby gas leaks or hazmat releases, derailments with releases, unhealthy air, Class I recalls of common foods, and large demonstrations closing major roads today.
  - Next is what's worth knowing this week: new recalls, main breaks, big road closures, DUI checkpoints, planned protests, kids' product warnings, algal bloom changes, claim deadlines, and sky events.
  - Background items come last.
- **Email layout (one email covering everything):**
  1. A one- or two-sentence summary saying whether anything needs action today, across all topics.
  2. **Needs attention:** most urgent first, from any topic, each with what to do.
  3. **Worth knowing (Toledo):** grouped by topic.
  4. **Emerging Tech Watch:** Geoengineering, Smart Dust, and 6G Cellular subsections.
  5. **All clear / no change:** one bullet per quiet section. Maumee River, Lake Erie, and Air are always listed, and so is any quiet Emerging Tech topic.
  6. **Sky:** a lighter closing note.
- **Bullets, one email, long lists linked (C T's requests, Oct 4 and Oct 9, 2026):**
  - Every item is its own bullet, with sub-bullets for what happened, where, dates and times, who's affected, the product name and brand, what to do, and the source link. No paragraph blocks. Skip empty headings.
  - Every item appears in the email, including ongoing ones, marked "(ongoing)" or "(updated)". Never drop an item.
  - Keep the email to ONE message under about 25,000 characters so it always sends. Long lot-code, UPC, NDC, model or serial-number lists (more than about three codes), long exposure-site lists, and extended background go ONLY in the full digest file. In the email, write "Lot codes and full details: <link>" pointing to that day's file.
  - The full file is saved as /workspace/toledo-safety-digest/digest-YYYY-MM-DD.txt and committed and pushed to the repo BEFORE the email is sent. The link format is https://github.com/coattails-droid/toledo-safety-digest/blob/main/digest-YYYY-MM-DD.txt
  - Before sending, check that the email body is under the limit and contains no placeholder text. Send exactly once, after checking Sent so it can't be duplicated.
- Use plain language, with a source link on every item.

## Rules

- Use reliable sources: agencies and local news such as the Toledo Blade, WTOL, 13abc, WTVG, the City of Toledo, Lucas County EMA, and Ohio EPA.
- Never invent incidents, readings, closures, checkpoints, sightings, events, or cases.
