NEEDS ATTENTION
- UPDATED: North Toledo chemical release (MC Tank, 1266 Progress Ave.) - all orders lifted; clean-up steps for homes in the plume area
  - What happened: about 5,500 gallons of glycidyl methacrylate vented from a tank starting Tue Oct 20; the mayor says an employee error during a transfer caused a chemical reaction. Shelter-in-place lifted Wed 5 p.m.; evacuation cut to 1/2 mile Wed, 1/4 mile Thu; tank finished venting Thu night; last evacuation lifted Fri Oct 23 at noon (about 152 people). Site returned to the company Fri evening for clean-up under U.S. EPA and Ohio EPA oversight.
  - Air: EPA's TAGA mobile lab (bus moving about 5 mph through the area) found no detectable glycidyl methacrylate in surrounding neighborhoods in raw data from Fri evening on; monitoring and lab sampling continue. An independent tester (Scott Smith) says his lab results will take 3-5 weeks.
  - If your home was in the plume: open windows on opposite sides to air it out; wipe surfaces with warm water and dish soap while wearing gloves; throw away uncovered food, pet food or water left out during the release (closed fridge/freezer food may be fine); if eyes, skin or breathing get irritated indoors, go outside to fresh air and get medical advice, saying you may have been exposed to GMA; 911 for severe breathing trouble.
  - Help: Health Department chemical-leak hotline 419-779-7089; shelter needs 1-800-RED-CROSS or 211.
  - Sources: https://toledo.oh.gov/news/2026/10/24/hazmat-evacuation-in-north-toledo and https://lucascountyhealth.com/public-health-update-glycidyl-methacrylate-gma-chemical-leak/ and https://www.wtol.com/article/news/local/epa-continues-air-monitoring-after-glycidyl-methacrylate-leak-in-north-toledo-ohio-chemical-air-quality/512-9bbed01a-d454-4a75-9567-2b79d76d8e52
