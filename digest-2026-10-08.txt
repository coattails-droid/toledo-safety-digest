- Babies' Magic Tea Baby Sleep Gripe Water - undeclared ethanol, methanol and isopropanol (NEW; company announced Oct 6, FDA posted Oct 7)
  - What happened: Pacific Health Sciences (Los Angeles County, CA) is voluntarily recalling one lot after FDA testing found alcohols not listed on the label: about 8.3% ethanol, 0.35% methanol and 0.48% isopropanol. The risk depends on how much was given and the child's age and weight. No adverse events reported to the company so far.
  - Product and lot: Babies' Magic Tea brand Baby Sleep Gripe Water dietary supplement, 4 fl oz (about 118 mL) amber bottle in a blue cardboard box, with an oral syringe; lot 25122bw, best before 12/2028. The lot is on the bottom of the box and on a side panel of the bottle.

  - What to do: stop giving it to your child; throw it away (bag it securely) or return it to the seller for a refund. If a child has symptoms after a dose, get medical care right away; for questions about a past dose, call your pediatrician or Poison Control at 1-800-222-1222 (24/7, free).

      - Pagliai's Restaurant, Sep 23, 5 to 9 p.m.

  - "Benadryl Challenge" / "Benny tripping" warning (ongoing; New Jersey Poison Control Center, Oct 6; Michigan Poison & Drug Information Center has issued the same warning)

  - EU opens a tender to detect rogue sun-blocking efforts (updated; Bloomberg via Spokesman-Review Oct 6, Business Standard Oct 7)
    - What happened: the EU's Horizon Europe research program launched a call worth about $39.4 million (reported as about 35 million euros) to fund research and coordination on solar radiation modification, including a monitoring framework to "detect and attribute outdoor experiments and deter potential unilateral deployments by private or state actors."
    - What it isn't: the tender explicitly excludes deployment and field experiments; it's about governance and detection.
    - Context: this follows a UN warning last month that warming will cross 1.5 C (2.7 F) within a few years; some scientists back an international non-use agreement for SRM.
    - Confirmed vs. claimed: the tender is an official EU call (confirmed). It is not an EU plan to block the sun, so watch for posts framing it that way.
    - Added detail (Business Standard, Oct 7): the call specifically covers stratospheric aerosol injection, marine cloud brightening and cirrus or mixed-phase cloud changes, and it asks how governments could spot an intervention, trace where it came from and judge its effects.
